6th Grade Girls · 7v7

Tiger's Playbook

One formation, 4 plays, simple so the girls can call their own plays.

Team Items

  • This playbook is intended to provide structure to enable the girls to lead the team independently. We've kept it as short & simple as possible.
  • Our only ask is that the girls have a good attitude and be good teammates.
  • We have an extremely talented group of girls. Everyone who wants to carry the ball will have opportunities, but likely not every game.
  • Quarterbacks will be a more significant commitment this year since coaches will not be on the field. They will call their own plays and keep everyone focused & organized. Please ensure your daughter understands the extra commitment prior to asking to try QB.
  • Defense will also have a captain who will assign rushers each play.
  • We need to get at least 7 girls to each game. We'll send a message gameday morning — please show up if you RSVP.

Rules Everyone Should Know

  • On offense, 3 players must be on the line of scrimmage — the center (C) and two linemen (A, B). The remaining 4 players (QB, RB, X, Y) are set back off the line.
  • On defense, players can line up anywhere behind the line. 2 players can rush each play — those 2 girls must be 5 yards off the line. All defenders can rush once the ball is handed off or the ball carrier makes a forward motion.
  • Scoring: Touchdown = 6 points, Extra point run = 1 point, Extra point pass = 2 points.
  • Punts (or "throws"): Dead ball when it hits the ground. If it's caught in the air it can be returned.
  • Interceptions are live and can be returned, except on an extra point.
  • All offensive players on the field are eligible to catch a pass.
  • If a flag falls out without being pulled, play continues until the ball carrier is touched. This happened a lot last year (and I just learned the rule).
Offense

Offensive Positions

RoleDescription
CCenter — always on the line, snaps the ball.
A & BA & B are also on the line. A left of center, B right of center.
QBAlways lines up directly behind C. Can throw, hand off, or run.
RBOne dedicated running back each play, lined up directly behind QB in a straight line, unless it's "Trips" formation.
X & YWide receivers, one yard off the line. X is typically wide left and Y wide right.

Formation

A/B stay tight to C, X/Y sit at the midpoint between A/B and the sideline, RB directly behind QB.

A C B X QB Y RB

Adding Twins or Trips

Twins and Trips are tags you add to shift X, Y (and RB, for Trips) to one side. They don't change where A, B, C, or QB line up — just where X, Y, and RB release from:

  • Twins [Side] — X & Y both shift to the called side. RB stays home.
  • Trips [Side] — X, Y & RB all shift to the called side. QB stands alone behind C.

A C B QB X Y RB

Trips Left

A C B RB X Y QB

How to Call a Play

Play → Side → "Down, Set, Hike" "Run - Right" · "Pass - Right"
  1. First say the Play (Run, QB Pop, Reverse, Pass), then the side (Right, Left).
  2. Snap is always on "Hike," but players cannot move until the ball actually moves, so players need to be watching the hike.
  3. Keep the huddle quick! At 30 seconds it's a delay of game penalty.
  4. Quarterbacks call plays and play a full half (2 QBs per game). We will have wrist band cheat sheets to wear if wanted.

Adding Twins or Trips

If using Twins or Trips, add it to the front of the call: Twins/Trips + Side → Play → Side → "Down, Set, Hike". The Twins/Trips "side" and the play "side" do not need to match.

"Twins Right - Run - Left"

Run Plays

Handoff RUN

QB pivots, hands ball to RB. RB reads the line and makes one cut inside or outside.

Call: "Run - Right"

Quick keeper QB POP

Quick QB sneak off the snap. QB can call a side or just read the play live.

Call: "QB Pop" or "QB Pop - Right"

Misdirection REVERSE

Always called as two parts: QB fakes a handoff to RB going one direction (Fake Run), then reverses field and hands off to X or Y going the other direction (Reverse).

Call: "Fake Run - Right - Reverse - Left" → fake to RB goes right, ball actually goes left to X or Y

Adding a Fake

The QB can add "Fake" in front of Run or Pass to sell that action, then keep the ball and run it herself — the fake and her run are the same side.

Call order: Fake [Run/Pass] → Side → "QB Keep"

"Fake Run - Right - QB Keep" → fake and QB both go right

"Fake Pass - Right - QB Keep" → fake pass look goes right, QB keeps and runs right

Fake Reverse follows the same two-part pattern as a real Reverse, just with a fake instead of an actual handoff, then the QB keeps and continues whichever way that last fake was headed.

"Fake Run - Right - Fake Reverse - Left - QB Keep" → fake to RB goes right, fake reverse goes left, QB keeps it and runs left

Routes

SLANT

2 steps up field, then a sharp cut inside.

OUT

5 yards up field, then break hard to the sideline.

HOOK

5 yards up field, then stop and turn back to the QB.

POST

Release deep, then bend toward the middle of the field.

Pass Play

Default PASS

By default, every eligible receiver — A, B, X & Y — runs a Post. RB is always the built-in screen outlet, releasing to the called side as the safety valve if nothing's open downfield.

Call: "Pass - Right"

The QB can change any receiver's route by naming that position plus the route she wants instead — anyone not called out just stays on Post.

Call order: Pass → Side → (Position Route, Position Route, ...)

"Pass Right, Y Hook" → Y runs Hook, A/B/X stay on Post

"Pass Right, B Out, Y Slant" → B runs Out, Y runs Slant, A/X stay on Post

Extra Points

No special plays — call any normal play from the regular playbook. A run play in = 1 point, a pass play in = 2 points.

Defense

Defensive Positions

RoleDescription
Line (x2)Anchor the point of attack, reading run or pass first. No requirement to line up across from any specific offensive player.
Rushers (x2)Must line up 5 yards off the line of scrimmage. Can rush or drop into coverage, decided by the play call.
Flankers (x2)Set up wide, guard the sidelines so nobody gets outside them.
Free Safety (x1)Reads the play. Since the Flankers seal the outside in, the Free Safety plays it the opposite way — pushing runners from the inside out toward the sideline help. Reads the quarterback if it develops into a pass.

Defensive Calls

  • "ZONE" — everyone drops to an area and covers whoever enters it. With only 2 max rushers (5 yards off the ball), zone is easy to teach and forgiving of mistakes.
  • Defenders can't converge on the ball carrier until the handoff/pitch/forward motion actually happens — no one should be sprinting in from off the line before that.
